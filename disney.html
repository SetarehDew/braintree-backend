Walt Disney
December 5, 1901 – December 15, 1966

The Man Behind the Magic

Walter Elias Disney was born on December 5, 1901, in Chicago, Illinois. From an early age, Walt showed a natural talent for drawing and storytelling, spending hours sketching the world around him. His passion for art and animation would go on to transform the entertainment industry forever.

In 1923, Walt and his brother Roy O. Disney founded the Disney Brothers Cartoon Studio in Hollywood, California. What began as a small animation workshop would grow into one of the most recognized and beloved entertainment companies in the world.

Walt's most iconic creation, Mickey Mouse, debuted in 1928 in "Steamboat Willie" — one of the first synchronized sound cartoons ever produced. Mickey Mouse became an instant sensation and remains one of the most recognized characters in history.

Beyond cartoons, Walt Disney pioneered the concept of the feature-length animated film with Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs in 1937 — a project many in Hollywood called "Disney's Folly." It became the highest-grossing film of its time and proved that animation could tell powerful, emotional stories for audiences of all ages.

Walt Disney received 22 Academy Awards throughout his career — more than any other individual in history. His legacy lives on not just in his films and theme parks, but in the timeless belief that with enough imagination, anything is possible.

The Disney Legacy

1923

The Disney Brothers Cartoon Studio is founded in Hollywood, California by Walt and Roy Disney. Their first production, the Alice Comedies series, combines live action with animation.

1928

Mickey Mouse makes his debut in "Steamboat Willie," the first cartoon with synchronized sound. Mickey becomes an instant worldwide sensation.

1937

Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs becomes the world's first feature-length cel-animated film. Critics called it "Disney's Folly" — it went on to become the highest-grossing film of 1938.

1940s–1950s

Disney releases a string of beloved classics including Pinocchio, Fantasia, Dumbo, Bambi, Cinderella, and Peter Pan, cementing its place as the world leader in animated storytelling.

1955

Disneyland opens in Anaheim, California — the world's first theme park of its kind. Walt called it "the happiest place on Earth." Over 28,000 guests attended on opening day.

1966

Walt Disney passes away on December 15, 1966. His brother Roy Disney continues his legacy, overseeing the completion of Walt Disney World in Florida.

1971

Walt Disney World opens in Orlando, Florida — the largest theme park resort in the world, fulfilling Walt's final dream.

1989–Present

The Disney Renaissance brings a new golden age with beloved films like The Little Mermaid, Beauty and the Beast, The Lion King, and Aladdin. Disney continues to grow through acquisitions of Pixar, Marvel, and Lucasfilm.
