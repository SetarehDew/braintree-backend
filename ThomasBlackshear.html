About the Artist

Born: November 14, 1955, in Waco, Texas; raised in Atlanta, Georgia.

Education: Studied at the Art Institute of Chicago before transferring to the American Academy of Art in Chicago, from which he graduated in 1977.

Artistic Style: Blackshear is celebrated for his dramatic lighting, emotional depth, and sensitivity to mood. He coined the term "Western Nouveau" for his distinctive style — a blend of art nouveau fluid lines with Western and Native cultural imagery, using plant-inspired forms, muted earthy palettes, and organic shapes.

Notable Works: Illustrated over 30 U.S. postage stamps, including contributions to the Black Heritage series featuring Jean Baptiste Point du Sable, James W. Johnson, Asa Philip Randolph, and Ida B. Wells. A touring exhibition of his Black Heritage series debuted at the Smithsonian's National Museum of American History in 1992.

Thomas Blackshear is known for creating expressive, richly detailed artwork and collectible pieces with emotional depth, elegance, and powerful storytelling. His work brings together beauty, character, and meaning — making each piece a memorable addition for collectors who appreciate fine artistry.
